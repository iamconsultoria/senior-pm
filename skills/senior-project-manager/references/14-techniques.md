## Estimating
Analogous estimating · parametric estimating · bottom-up estimating · multipoint estimating
(three-point) · constructive cost model (COCOMO) · estimation techniques · velocity ·
reserve analysis

Analogous is fast and coarse; parametric scales with a driver; bottom-up is accurate and
expensive; multipoint carries uncertainty explicitly. **Match the method to the decision the
estimate supports.** A gate decision needs a range, not a number — and a single-point estimate
presented to a sponsor becomes a commitment whether you meant it or not.

## Scheduling
Critical path method `Figure 5-4, p. 161` · critical path drag · critical path drag cost ·
critical chain method · critical chain project management (CCPM) · precedence diagramming
method `Figure 5-13, p. 186` · logical relationship · leads and lags `Figure 5-12, p. 180` ·
dependency determination and integration · schedule network analysis · schedule compression
`Figure 5-20, p. 196` · resource leveling `Figure 5-18, p. 193` · resource optimization
technique · rolling wave planning · agile release planning `Figure 5-1, p. 147` · theory of
constraints (TOC)

**Critical path drag** is underused and worth knowing: it quantifies how much a specific
critical activity is extending the project — the answer to "where do we actually compress?"
rather than "who can go faster?"

**Compression:** crashing adds cost, fast-tracking adds risk and rework. Neither creates time;
both convert one constraint into another. State the conversion when recommending it.

## Financial analysis
Earned value (EV) analysis `Table 5-1, p. 207` · to-complete performance index (TCPI)
`Figure 5-24, p. 206` · cost aggregation · cost-benefit analysis · cost of quality (CoQ)
`Figure 5-3, p. 159` · funding limit reconciliation · financing · reserve analysis ·
variance analysis · trend analysis · regression analysis · predictive analytics

**TCPI** answers the question EVM users usually stop short of: what performance is now required
to finish within budget? A CPI of 0.9 with a TCPI of 1.4 is a project that will not recover, and
saying so early is more useful than reporting the variance again.

## Risk analysis
Probability and impact matrix `Figure 5-14, p. 187` · risk probability and impact assessment ·
risk categorization · prompt lists · assumption and constraint analysis · root cause analysis ·
sensitivity analysis and tornado diagram `Figure 5-21, p. 198` · simulation and Monte Carlo
`Figure 5-22, p. 199` · decision tree analysis `Figure 5-5, p. 164` · influence diagrams ·
what-if analysis · strategies for threats · strategies for opportunities · contingent response
strategies · strategies for overall project risk

**Strategies for overall project risk** is the one to remember. The standard expects a response
posture for aggregate exposure, not only individual lines. That is the difference between a
risk register and risk management.
