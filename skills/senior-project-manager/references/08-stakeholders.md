# Stakeholders — §2.5, pp. 67–78

Absorbed the former Communications knowledge area. **Seven processes** across Initiating,
Planning, Executing and Monitoring/Controlling. Key Concepts p. 68 · Processes p. 70 ·
Tailoring p. 74 · Interactions p. 77 · Check p. 78. Overview: Figure 2-32, p. 70. Examples of
stakeholders: Figure 2-31, p. 68.

## Processes

**Identify Stakeholders** (I, p. 71) — identifying stakeholders **regularly**, not once. One of
only two Initiating processes in the standard. Stakeholder landscapes shift with
reorganisations, contract changes and political weather; a register dated at kickoff is a
historical document.

**Plan Stakeholder Engagement** (P, p. 72) — strategies for engaging those identified.
**Plan Communications Management** (P, p. 73) — who gets what, when, how, in what form.
**Manage Stakeholder Engagement** (E, p. 74) — communicating and working with them.
**Manage Communications** (E, p. 75) — timely and appropriate collection and distribution.
**Monitor Stakeholder Engagement** (M, p. 76) — tracking engagement levels against target.
**Monitor Communications** (M, p. 77) — verifying information needs are actually being met.

**The parallel structure is deliberate.** Engagement and communications each get a plan, a
manage and a monitor process. They are separate tracks. Broadcasting well to a disengaged
stakeholder is not engagement, and this domain structurally refuses to let you conflate them.
When a sponsor says "they were informed," ask which track they mean.

## Tailoring — §2.5.3, p. 74
The longest tailoring section of any domain (pp. 74–77). Calibrate on: number and diversity of
stakeholders · geographic and cultural spread · regulatory reporting obligations · supplier and
partner involvement · the organisation's escalation culture.

Virtual and distributed contexts change communications *design*, not just tooling. Time-zone
overlap determines what can be synchronous, which determines what can be decided quickly.

## Interactions — §2.5.4, p. 77
**→ Governance:** who sits in which decision forum is an output of stakeholder analysis, not an
org-chart accident.
**→ Scope:** acceptance authority settled before Validate Scope.
**→ Risk:** a disengaged or hostile stakeholder is a risk with an owner and a response, not a
grievance.
**→ Resources:** suppliers are stakeholders and resources simultaneously; keep both views.

## Check outcomes — Table 2-9, p. 78 (restated)
1. **Engagement maintained** — feedback via interviews or surveys, periodic re-alignment on
   objectives, indicators such as Net Promoter Score.
2. **Risk responses identified and implemented** — issue log, risk register and stakeholder
   register reviewed *together*.
3. **Agreement with project objectives** — number of change requests to requirements, plus
   feedback collected on increments.
4. **Communications management performed** — the plan reviewed periodically and re-tailored to
   stakeholder needs, not written once and filed.
5. **Plans integrated with suppliers' perspectives and work outputs** — the project management
   plan aligns with the vendor's way of working.

Item 5 is easy to miss and expensive to discover late: it asks whether your plan and your
supplier's operating model are actually compatible — cadence, reporting, definition of done —
not whether the contract is signed.

## The pattern to watch for
Green on Scope, Schedule and Finance with red on Stakeholders is the most expensive pattern in
the whole check set: on track by every internal measure, and heading for rejection at
acceptance. If you see it, stop reporting status and go fix the relationship.
