# Finance — §2.4, pp. 58–67

Replaced Cost, deliberately wider: funding, revenue, value and financial visibility, not just
cost control. Four processes, all Planning and Monitoring/Controlling. Key Concepts p. 59 ·
Processes p. 61 · Tailoring p. 65 · Interactions p. 66 · Check p. 66. Overview: Figure 2-26,
p. 62. Budget buildup scenarios: Figure 2-25, p. 61.

## Processes

**Plan Financial Management** (P, p. 63) — how revenues and costs will be planned, structured,
managed and controlled. Note *revenues* — this domain covers the money coming in, not only
going out. On client-funded work that is the whole game.

**Estimate Costs** (P, p. 63) — approximating the cost of resources required. Techniques:
analogous, parametric, bottom-up, multipoint (three-point), COCOMO for software. Always carry
the **basis of estimates** — an estimate without its assumptions cannot be defended or updated.

**Develop Budget** (P, p. 64) — aggregating estimates into an authorised cost baseline. Cost
aggregation, reserve analysis, funding limit reconciliation, financing.

**Monitor and Control Finances** (M, p. 65) — financial status, change management, forecasting.
EV analysis, variance analysis, trend analysis, TCPI, reserve analysis.

**Cost baseline vs funding requirements are different lines** `→ Figure 4-1, p. 126`. This is
why projects "have budget" and still cannot pay an invoice this quarter. Check both.

## Tailoring — §2.4.3, p. 65
Calibrate on funding model (capex/opex, staged vs annual, internal vs client-funded),
contractual structure, and how much financial authority sits with the PM. In adaptive settings
funding is often per-increment or per-team-period — you estimate **capacity**, not the backlog.
Trying to cost an emergent backlog is a category error; fund the team, cap the horizon.

## Interactions — §2.4.4, p. 66
**→ Governance:** authorisation limits and funding gates.
**→ Schedule:** the schedule shapes the funding curve.
**→ Risk:** contingency and management reserve are financial instruments before they are risk
instruments. Who releases them, on what evidence, is a governance decision — settle it in the
charter.
**→ Scope:** the business case is the anchor. When scope changes and the business case is not
re-tested, the project is running on a stale value proposition.

## Check outcomes — Table 2-8, p. 67 (restated)
1. **Contributes to business objectives and strategy (value maximisation)** — ROI, NPV, IRR,
   cost-benefit analysis, KPIs, OKRs, capex and opex position.
2. **Completion within or below budget** — variance analysis via cost variance and cost
   performance index, plus achievement of financial targets with vendors under the signed
   contract.
3. **Deliverables validated against plan** — EVM plus organisation-defined metrics.
4. **Value created financially, or as another defined kind of value** — measured against the
   success criteria defined for *this* project, which may be investment or future potential
   rather than in-period return.
5. **Financial visibility achieved** — trend or graphical analysis and forecasting in place.

Item 4 carries more weight than it looks. The standard permits value defined as something other
than in-period return — **but only if defined in the success criteria up front**
`→ Standard §2.1.2, p. 16`. Retrofitting a value definition after an overrun is not tailoring,
it is a story. Call it when you see it.
