# Resources — §2.6, pp. 79–92

Team, physical and virtual resources together. Five processes across Planning, Executing and
Monitoring/Controlling. Key Concepts p. 79 · Processes p. 80 · Tailoring p. 88 · Interactions
p. 91 · Check p. 92. Overview: Figure 2-40, p. 80.

## Processes

**Plan Resource Management** (P, p. 81) — how to estimate, acquire, lead and control resources.
Outputs include the resource management plan and team charter.

**Estimate Resources** (P, p. 82) — team resources plus type and quantity of material,
equipment and supplies. Resource breakdown structure: `→ §4, Figure 4-4, p. 133`.

**Acquire Resources** (E, p. 83) — obtaining team, physical or virtual resources. Preassignment,
negotiation, virtual teams.

**Lead the Team** (E, p. 85) — guiding, developing and managing the team to improve
performance. **Leadership is a process here, with inputs and techniques and outputs — not a
soft skill in an appendix.** That placement is the standard's clearest statement that leading
people is project work.

**Monitor and Control Resourcing** (M, p. 87) — availability against plan, utilisation
management.

## Techniques
Responsibility assignment matrix and RACI `→ §5, Figure 5-19, p. 195` · hierarchical charts ·
text-oriented formats · team building and the Tuckman ladder · individual and team assessments ·
emotional intelligence Figure 5-11, p. 178 · conflict management · motivation · coaching and
mentoring · training · recognition and rewards · servant leadership · centralized vs
distributed management and leadership · ground rules · colocation · virtual teams · resource
levelling p. 193 and resource optimization · preassignment · negotiation · green human resource
management · resource-based view.

Artifacts: team charter · skill matrix · project team assignments · physical or virtual resource
assignments · resource calendar · team performance assessments.

**On the team charter:** it works when the team writes it, or at minimum contributes to it. A
team charter issued to a team is a policy document, and gets the compliance a policy document
gets.

## Tailoring — §2.6.3, p. 88
Long section (pp. 88–91). Calibrate on: dedicated vs matrixed team · co-located vs distributed ·
employee vs contractor mix · physical resource intensity · how much authority the PM actually
has over selection and release.

Centralised vs distributed decision-making is treated explicitly. Self-organising teams still
need defined decision boundaries — autonomy without a boundary produces either paralysis or a
surprise.

## Interactions — §2.6.4, p. 91
**→ Schedule:** durations depend on who is assigned at what allocation. This is the single most
common source of schedule fiction.
**→ Finance:** resource plans are the largest cost driver on most projects.
**→ Governance:** sourcing strategy is decided in Governance, executed here.

## Check outcomes — Table 2-10, p. 92 (restated)
1. **Shared ownership** — team members know the vision and objectives and own the deliverables
   and outcomes.
2. **High-performing team in place** — trust and collaboration; adapts to change and is
   resilient under pressure; members feel empowered and are recognised; results delivered.
3. **Effective resource utilisation** — actual versus planned usage managed.
4. **Acceptable resource-downtime percentage** — total downtime against total planned resource
   time.
5. **Team is productive** — units produced per hours worked, or cost of work performed against
   scheduled time, at acceptable levels, with KPIs reviewed regularly.
6. **Efficient physical and virtual resource management** — materials used, scrap discarded and
   rework volumes indicate efficient use.
7. **Resource procurement managed** — process, efficiency checks, ordering, manufacture and
   delivery tracking all handled.

Items 1–2 are qualitative, 3–7 quantitative, and the domain requires both. A team hitting every
utilisation target while nobody owns the outcome fails this check — and utilisation targets are
exactly what a struggling PM reaches for. Watch for it.
