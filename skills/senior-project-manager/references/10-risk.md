# Risk — §2.7, pp. 92–102

Threats and opportunities together. Six processes — **four in Planning**, which tells you how
front-loaded the standard considers risk work. Key Concepts p. 92 · Processes p. 94 · Tailoring
p. 98 · Interactions p. 101 · Check p. 102. Overview: Figure 2-47, p. 95. Risk classification:
Figure 2-46, p. 93.

## Processes

**Plan Risk Management** (P, p. 96) — how risk activities will be conducted, established early.
Sets thresholds, categories, roles, and the probability/impact definitions. Definitions table:
`→ §4, Table 4-2, p. 136`. Risk breakdown structure: Figure 4-5, p. 135.

**Identify Risks** (P, p. 97) — threats **and** opportunities. Ongoing, not an event. Prompt
lists, checklists, assumption and constraint analysis, root cause analysis, SWOT, document
analysis, brainstorming, interviews.

**Perform Risk Analysis** (P, p. 98) — iterative. **Merges the former qualitative and
quantitative processes into one**, which is a real simplification: you assess, and you go
deeper where depth is warranted, rather than running two ceremonies. Probability and impact
assessment, risk categorization, probability and impact matrix p. 187, simulation p. 199,
sensitivity analysis and tornado diagram p. 198, decision tree p. 164, influence diagrams.

**Plan Risk Responses** (P, p. 99) — strategies for threats, strategies for opportunities,
contingent response strategies, strategies for overall project risk. Note the fourth: the
standard expects a response posture for the project's *aggregate* risk, not only line items.

**Implement Risk Responses** (E, p. 100) — actually executing them. **The existence of this as
a separate accountable process is pointed.** Registers full of planned responses that nobody
executes are the standard failure mode. When you review a register, check the implemented
column before you read anything else.

**Monitor Risks** (M, p. 100) — tracking, analysing, executing response plans, evaluating
effectiveness, reserve analysis, audits.

## Tailoring — §2.7.3, p. 98
Long section (pp. 98–101). Calibrate on: complexity and novelty · regulatory exposure ·
organisational risk appetite · contractual risk allocation · delivery approach.

Adaptive approaches absorb some risk structurally through short feedback cycles — **but only
risks that short cycles surface.** Strategic, regulatory, supply-chain and geopolitical risks
are not among them, and "we're agile so we handle risk in the sprint" is a common and expensive
error. Say so.

Resilience is treated as buildable rather than innate — see the resilience entries in §5.

## Interactions — §2.7.4, p. 101
**→ Finance:** contingency and management reserve. Release authority is a governance decision.
**→ Schedule:** floats, buffers, secondary network strategies.
**→ Governance:** escalation thresholds — where a risk stops being the PM's.
**→ Stakeholders:** stakeholder posture is a risk category with an owner.
**→ Scope:** unclear requirements are a risk source, not just a scope defect.

## Check outcomes — Table 2-11, p. 102 (restated)
1. **Environmental awareness** — technical, social, political, market and economic context
   incorporated when evaluating uncertainty, risks and responses.
2. **Proactive exploration of and response to uncertainty** — responses aligned with budget,
   schedule and performance constraints.
3. **Capacity to anticipate threats and opportunities** — a process for identifying, assessing,
   documenting and responding that is well understood **by the team**, not only the PM.
4. **Delivery with minimal impact from unknown events** — reserves in place and used, dates
   met, budget performance within variance thresholds.
5. **Opportunities realised** — established mechanisms to identify, leverage and track
   opportunity realisation. **Most registers track threats only and fail this outright.**
6. **Contingency reserves used effectively** — the team proactively prevents threats, limiting
   reserve consumption.
7. **Resilience and recovery capability developed** — the team knows the organisation's business
   continuity or emergency response plan; a project continuity plan exists where applicable; a
   management reserve covers unknown risks; and under crisis the team can restructure quickly to
   adapt to new constraints.

Item 7 is the newest in emphasis and the one to raise on any programme with operational,
regulatory or geopolitical exposure. It asks whether the team could reorganise under crisis —
a question almost no risk register answers, and one that separates a register from actual risk
management.
