# The six principles — as a decision lens

`→ Standard §3, pp. 35–55`. Consolidated from twelve in the 7th edition; mapping at
Table X5-2, p. 259.

The mindset framing (§3.1, p. 36) has two dimensions — proactive and ownership — which is the
whole standard's posture in miniature: anticipate rather than react, own rather than escalate.

Each principle has three parts in the book: Project Impact · Principle in Action · Connected
Performance Domains. The third is the only one that changes what you do.

---

## 1. Adopt a Holistic View — §3.3, p. 38
The project is a system inside a larger system. Decisions ripple: a scope concession changes
the risk profile and the benefits case, not just the backlog.

**Bites hardest on:** Governance, Risk.
**In practice:** before committing to any change, name what else moves.
**Failure mode:** analysis that never converges. Holism is a check, not a licence to widen
indefinitely.

## 2. Focus on Value — §3.4, p. 40
Outcomes over outputs. Delivering everything on the list is not success if the benefit never
materialises.

**Bites on:** Finance, Scope, Governance.
**In practice:** every deliverable names the outcome it serves and who confirms it landed.
**Failure mode:** "value" as a rhetorical device for skipping anything inconvenient.

## 3. Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables — §3.5, p. 43
Quality is built in, not inspected in. Note the placement — this principle absorbed the old
Quality knowledge area. The only surviving quality *process* is Manage Quality Assurance,
inside Governance, and it is process-focused rather than deliverable-inspection.

**Bites on:** Governance, Scope.
**In practice:** ask what in the *process* produced the defect. Table 3-1, p. 112 says the same
— fix the process, don't add verification steps.
**Failure mode:** gold-plating wearing quality's badge.

## 4. Be an Accountable Leader — §3.6, p. 46
Ownership of outcomes, ethical conduct, decisions made and owned rather than diffused across a
committee.

**Bites on:** Governance, Stakeholders, Resources.
**In practice:** every open decision has a named owner and a date. Not a forum — a person.
**Failure mode:** accountability collapsing into control.

## 5. Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas — §3.7, p. 48
The most substantially new principle in this edition. Environmental, social and economic
dimensions treated as project areas rather than a compliance annex. Sustainability Pyramid:
Figure 3-7, p. 49. How it broadens the orientation of project management: Figure 3-8, p. 51.

**Bites on:** Scope, Finance, Governance.
**In practice:** the guide expects sustainability work to appear **in the WBS or backlog** —
it shows up explicitly as a Scope check outcome (Table 2-6, p. 46), with examples like managing
emissions from project activity or biodiversity impact where material. That is an auditable
test, not a values statement.
**Failure mode:** a charter paragraph no work package traces to. When a sponsor asks how
sustainability is "actually" handled, go straight to the WBS question.

## 6. Build an Empowered Culture — §3.8, p. 53
Teams that own their work, decide within their remit, and are safe to surface bad news early.

**Bites on:** Resources, Stakeholders.
**In practice:** compare where decisions actually get made against where they are supposed to
be made. Table 3-1, p. 112 prescribes delegated authority thresholds when approvals bottleneck.
**Failure mode:** empowerment without capability or context — abdication with better branding.

## The tensions — this is the part that matters

Recited, principles are wallpaper. They earn their place when two point in opposite directions
and someone has to choose.

| Tension | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Focus on Value ↔ Embed Quality** | Cutting test cycles, dropping review gates, shipping the MVP. Which value, measured when? |
| **Accountable Leader ↔ Empowered Culture** | Who overrides a team estimate the PM believes is wrong? Escalation vs autonomy. |
| **Sustainability ↔ Focus on Value** | Whose value, over what horizon? In-period ROI vs lifecycle cost and externalities. |
| **Holistic View ↔ delivery pressure** | How much analysis before committing? Systems thinking has a cost and a clock. |
| **Focus on Value ↔ Holistic View** | Optimising one workstream's outcome while degrading the portfolio's. |
| **Embed Quality ↔ Empowered Culture** | Imposed standards vs team-owned definitions of done. |

## Using a tension in a decision

1. State the fork in one sentence.
2. Name the two principles in conflict.
3. Per option: what it privileges, what it sacrifices, **who bears the cost**.
4. Give a recommendation. Not a menu — a recommendation, with reasoning.
5. State what evidence would flip it.
6. Attach the check that will show, later, whether the call was right.

Steps 5 and 6 are what separate a decision record from an opinion. Almost everyone stops at 4,
which is why almost no organisation learns from its decisions.
