# Scope — §2.2, pp. 35–46

Six processes, **all Planning and Monitoring/Controlling — no Executing processes**. Key
Concepts p. 35 · Processes p. 39 · Tailoring p. 44 · Interactions p. 45 · Check p. 46.
Overview: Figure 2-13, p. 39.

Key concepts open with the **business case** — a documented economic feasibility study
establishing the validity of the benefits, outlining costs, benefits, how value is created, and
the success criteria. Scope traces back to it or it is not scope, it is activity.

## Processes

**Plan Scope Management** (P, p. 40) — how scope will be defined, validated, controlled.
Small projects skip this and regret it at the first change, because there is no agreed rule for
what counts as a change.

**Elicit and Analyze Requirements** (P, p. 41) — determining and documenting stakeholder needs.
The merged elicitation-plus-analysis framing is deliberate: capturing requirements without
analysing conflicts between them produces a document, not a specification. Traceability matrix:
Figure 4-3, p. 132.

**Define Scope** (P, p. 42) — detailed or high-level description of product and project scope.
Note "or high-level" — the standard explicitly permits scope defined at the boundary rather
than the item.

**Develop Scope Structure** (P, p. 43) — subdividing deliverables and work into manageable
components. WBS predictively, backlog structure adaptively. WBS samples: `→ §5` decomposed to
work packages p. 165, by phase p. 166, by major deliverable p. 167. Choose the organising axis
deliberately — it determines what your reporting can and cannot show.

**Monitor and Control Scope** (M, p. 43) — tracking status and managing baseline changes.
Produces *verified* deliverables.

**Validate Scope** (M, p. 44) — formalising customer acceptance of completed deliverables.

**The pairing at the end matters.** Monitor and Control Scope is internal verification; Validate
Scope is external acceptance. Verified deliverables feed into validation. Teams that conflate
them discover at UAT that "complete" meant complete to them only. Settle acceptance authority
before you need it, not during the acceptance meeting.

## Tailoring — §2.2.3, p. 44
Requirements stability is the pivot. Stable and understood → define up front, control by
baseline. Volatile or emergent → define the *boundary* and the *prioritisation mechanism*, not
the content; control by backlog and cadence.

Hybrid is the common real case: fixed outer boundary, adaptive interior. **Say in the scope
management plan which part is which.** Otherwise you get baseline arguments about the adaptive
half and unnoticed creep in the fixed half.

## Interactions — §2.2.4, p. 45
**→ Schedule, Finance:** every scope change is a schedule and cost event. No exceptions.
**→ Risk:** unclear requirements are a risk with an owner, not just an inconvenience.
**→ Stakeholders:** acceptance authority is a stakeholder question, decided before Validate
Scope.
**→ Governance:** the book flags Scope–Governance as often the most consequential domain pair.

## Check outcomes — Table 2-6, p. 46 (restated)
1. **Effective change management** — predictive: a change log showing each change's impact
   across Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources and Risk. Adaptive: a backlog
   showing completion rate, rate of scope addition, and how stakeholder feedback is prioritised
   and admitted.
2. **Clear understanding of requirements** — predictive: few changes to initial requirements
   indicates understanding. Evolving: each iteration delivers a well-defined short-term
   picture, with longer-term requirements refined progressively (rolling wave).
3. **Alignment with business objectives and strategy** — business case, strategic plan and
   authorising documents demonstrably line up with the deliverables.
4. **Stakeholders accept and are satisfied** — interviews, observation, end-user feedback;
   complaint and return levels as supporting indicators.
5. **Scope Definition Accuracy (%)** = planned scope items correctly delivered ÷ total planned
   scope items × 100.
6. **Scope Creep (%)** = total deliverables ÷ unplanned deliverables × 100.
7. **Requirements Stability (%)** = total requirements ÷ unchanged requirements × 100.
8. **Sustainability is in the scope** — the WBS or backlog contains actual activities (e.g.
   assessing and managing emissions from project activity, or biodiversity impact where
   material).

Item 8 is the auditable test of the sustainability principle. When a sponsor asks how
sustainability is really handled, this is the answer: show the work packages or admit there
are none.
