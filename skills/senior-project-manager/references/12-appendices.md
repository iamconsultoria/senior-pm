# Appendices X2–X5

## X2 — Project Management Offices (pp. 233–236)
Introduction 233 · **value proposition 233** · **customer centricity 234** · type, model and
structure 234 · **maturity models 235** · learn more 236.

The ordering is the argument: **value proposition and customer centricity come before
structure.** Use it against anyone who opens a PMO conversation with an org chart. Define who
the PMO's customers are and what they get, then choose the shape.

Use for: PMO design or redesign, maturity assessment, justifying or defending a PMO,
choosing between delivery / enabling / controlling models.

Pairs with Governance `→ §2.1` — a PMO is a governance instrument, and the governance check
outcomes `→ Table 2-5, p. 37` make a reasonable starting scorecard for one.

## X3 — Artificial Intelligence (pp. 237–244)
AI in the project context 237 · **adoption strategies 238** · state of the market 239 ·
**common use cases 239, Table X3-1, p. 240** · **responsible use and ethical concerns 239** ·
suggested resources 244.

Figure X3-1, p. 238 — how the underlying technologies relate to generative pretrained
transformers. **Figure X3-2, p. 243 — use cases plotted by complexity against need for human
intervention.** That figure is the practical one: it gives a defensible basis for deciding
which project tasks to automate and which stay human-in-the-loop, which is exactly the question
that gets asked and rarely answered rigorously.

The standard's position `→ Standard §1, p. 4`: AI can analyse large data volumes for insight,
predict risks, recommend options, automate routine work and improve forecasting — with impact
dependent on input quality and human oversight. It frames the payoff as freeing the PM for
strategic work, stakeholder engagement and continuous improvement.

Use for: AI adoption strategy in a delivery organisation or PMO, deciding what to automate,
governance and ethics guardrails, building a case to leadership.

## X4 — Procurement (pp. 245–254)
Introduction 245 · overview 246 · **make-or-buy 246** · strategy 247 · bid process and
documents 248 (flow: Figure X4-1, p. 248) · source selection analysis 249 · source selection
criteria 249 · **contract type 250** · fundamental contract models 251 · emerging trends 252 ·
**claims administration 252** · relevance to project managers 253 · sensitivity of legal actions
and upholding ethics codes 254.

No longer a knowledge area. Its hook into the domain structure is **Plan Sourcing Strategy** in
Governance `→ §2.1.6, p. 15`, with insourcing vs outsourcing tradeoffs at Table 2-3, p. 21.

**X4.9 claims administration** is the section most PMs have never read and eventually need. It
is explicit about the sensitivity of legal action and ethics obligations while a claim is live.
Read it before a dispute, not during one — once a claim is open, your correspondence becomes
evidence.

Use for: build-vs-buy, contract model selection, bid and source selection design, vendor
disputes, and any question about how much risk a contract actually transfers.

Pairs with: Governance (sourcing, authorisation limits) · Finance (vendor financial targets
appear in the Finance check outcomes) · Stakeholders (supplier alignment appears in the
Stakeholders check outcomes) · Risk (what the contract does and does not transfer).
