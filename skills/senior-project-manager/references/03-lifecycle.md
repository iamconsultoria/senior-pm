# Life cycles, approaches, cadence and focus areas

`→ Standard §4, pp. 57–74`

## Phases and boundaries
Phases §4.1, p. 57 · Project boundaries Figure 4-12, p. 71 · Impact of variables over time
Figure 4-1, p. 59 — the cost-of-change curve. Use it when you need to justify front-loading to
someone who wants to "start building."

Phase gates (p. 58) require **exit criteria defined before the phase starts.** Criteria
negotiated at the gate are not criteria; they are a conversation.

## Development approaches — §4.2, p. 59
A **spectrum**, not three boxes. Figure 4-2, p. 60.

**Predictive** (p. 61) — scope defined early, change controlled by baseline. Sample life cycle
Figure 4-4, p. 62; with incremental delivery Figure 4-5, p. 62.

**Adaptive** (p. 63) — scope emerges, controlled by prioritisation and cadence. Life cycle
Figure 4-6, p. 63. Constraint options Figure 4-3, p. 61. Agile approaches plotted by breadth
and depth Figure 4-7, p. 64.

**Hybrid** (p. 65) — four documented patterns. "We're hybrid" is not an answer; ask which:
1. Adaptive development then predictive rollout — Figure 4-8, p. 65
2. Adaptive and predictive simultaneously on different components — Figure 4-9, p. 66
3. Largely predictive with an adaptive component — Figure 4-10, p. 66
4. Largely adaptive with a predictive component — Figure 4-11, p. 67

Most organisations claiming hybrid are running pattern 3 while describing pattern 2. The
difference determines where your governance and your baselines sit.

## Selection — §4.3, p. 67
Three lenses, in this order:
1. **Deliverables** (p. 67) — novelty, stability, testability. *Decide on this first.*
2. **Project** (p. 68) — constraints, risk, delivery pressure.
3. **Organization** (p. 68) — culture, capability, structure, governance appetite.

Teams reliably invert this: they choose on organisational culture and retrofit a deliverable
justification. That is how you get an agile ceremony layer over a stage-gate reality, with the
worst properties of both.

## Delivery cadence — §4.4, p. 69
Single delivery · multiple deliveries · periodic deliveries. **Independent of development
approach.** A predictive project can deliver periodically; an adaptive one can deliver once.
Separating these two axes resolves most false "agile vs waterfall" arguments in an
organisation.

## Focus areas — §4.5, p. 69
The five process groups, restored under a new name and explicitly non-sequential.

Initiating 4.5.1 p. 70 · Planning 4.5.2 p. 70 · Executing 4.5.3 p. 72 · Monitoring and
Controlling 4.5.4 p. 72 · Closing 4.5.5 p. 72 · As a Whole 4.5.6 p. 73.

Interaction patterns: predictive Figure 4-13, p. 73 · **adaptive Figure 4-14, p. 74**. The
adaptive figure is the one to show sceptics — same five focus areas, cycling per iteration
rather than running once. It is the cleanest available argument that agile and the standard are
not in conflict.

## The crosswalk
`→ Guide Table 2-1, p. 9` maps all 40 processes to domain × focus area. Full listing in
`01-index.md`. The structural read:

- Initiating: **2 processes**
- Closing: **1 process**
- Executing: **6 processes**
- Everything else — 31 of 40 — is Planning or Monitoring and Controlling

The mass of the standard sits in planning and control. Two practical uses: push back on
kickoff workshops that try to cover everything, and push back on "we'll figure out governance
once we're running."
