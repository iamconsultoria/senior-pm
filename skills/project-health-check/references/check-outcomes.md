# The 44 check outcomes

Restated from the Check Outcomes tables, PMBOK Guide 8th Edition §2 (Tables 2-5 to 2-11).
Score each: 🟢 evidence seen · 🟡 partial or asserted · 🔴 absent · ⬜ N/A (justify).

---

## Governance — Table 2-5, p. 37 (5 checks)

**G1. Appropriate oversight by leadership and third-party stakeholders.**
Agreed processes, protocols or team agreements exist, are documented as appropriate, and align
with the organisation's other governance artifacts — policies, standards, procedures.
*Look for:* a governance model anyone can name without checking; alignment with corporate
policy rather than a parallel invented one.

**G2. Governance processes in place, effective, and continuously improved.**
Teams are aware of and act on governance structure, internal and external audit and compliance,
and customer governance requirements. Third-party governance is integrated with project
governance rather than running alongside it.
*Verify by:* compliance audits or checklists against internal/external standards in predictive
settings; regular use of the governance checklist, timeboxing, and held meetings in adaptive
ones.

**G3. Effective signalling mechanisms tied to target outcomes.**
One or more signalling mechanisms selected to fit how *this* project maximises its value
proposition.
*Look for:* early-warning indicators someone actually watches, not a report nobody reads.

**G4. Lessons learned and retrospectives managed throughout the project.**
Project and organisational knowledge combined to manage deliverables and value. Lessons
incorporated **during** the project, not only at the end.
*Red flag:* a lessons-learned session scheduled for closure and nothing before it.

**G5. Key management members kept apprised.**
Metrics in place and tracked regularly, covering: operational status and progress of components
and related activities; expected and incurred resource requirements; known risks with response
plans and escalation criteria; strategic and operational assumptions; benefits realised;
decision criteria, tracking and communication; change control; compliance with corporate and
legal policy; information and communications management; issues and issue response plans;
funding and financial performance.
*Use this list as the steering committee agenda.* Most packs cover two of the eleven.

## Scope — Table 2-6, p. 46 (8 checks)

**S1. Effective change management.**
Predictive: a change log demonstrating each change's impact across Scope, Schedule, Finance,
Stakeholders, Resources and Risk. Adaptive: a backlog managing scope, showing accomplishment
rate, rate of new scope added, and prioritisation and inclusion of stakeholder feedback.

**S2. Clear understanding of requirements.**
Predictive: fewer changes to initial requirements may reflect understanding. Evolving: each
iteration provides a clear, well-defined short-term picture, enabling rolling wave refinement
of longer-term requirements.

**S3. Alignment with business objectives and strategy.**
Business case, organisational strategic plan and authorising documents demonstrate that
deliverables and business objectives line up.

**S4. Stakeholders accept and are satisfied with deliverables.**
Interviews, observation and end-user feedback indicate satisfaction. Complaint and return
levels may serve as indicators.

**S5. Scope items clearly defined in the scope statement.**
Scope Definition Accuracy (%) = (planned scope items correctly delivered ÷ total planned scope
items) × 100.

**S6. Scope creep measured.**
Scope Creep (%) = (total deliverables ÷ unplanned deliverables) × 100.

**S7. Requirements stability measured.**
Requirements Stability (%) = (total requirements ÷ unchanged requirements) × 100.

**S8. Sustainability considered in project scope.**
The WBS or backlog includes activities to manage sustainability — for example assessing and
managing CO₂ emissions arising from project activity, or managing biodiversity impact where the
effect is significant.

## Schedule — Table 2-7, p. 58 (7 checks)

**SC1. Scheduling approaches consistent with the deliverables.** Schedule aligned with the
development approach — predictive, adaptive or hybrid.

**SC2. Life cycle consists of phases connecting value delivery end to end.** Work from launch
to close represented in phases, with appropriate exit criteria.

**SC3. Holistic approach to delivering outcomes.** The delivery schedule demonstrates the
project is planned holistically, with no gaps or areas of misalignment.

**SC4. Schedule documentation complete.** Essential elements present — task dependencies,
durations, resource allocations, milestones — at the level the chosen approach requires.

**SC5. Appropriate tools and techniques used.** E.g. project management software and critical
path method during schedule development.

**SC6. Stakeholders involved in schedule development.** Participation level from key
stakeholders — client, team members — checked. Low involvement predicts an unrealistic or
poorly developed schedule.

**SC7. Sufficient buffers for known-unknowns.** Major constraints and known risks factored in
through sufficient floats, contingency reserves, or alternate network strategies such as
secondary plans.

## Finance — Table 2-8, p. 67 (5 checks)

**F1. Contributes to business objectives and strategy (value maximisation).** Checked through
ROI, NPV, IRR, cost-benefit analysis, KPIs, OKRs, capex and opex position.

**F2. Completion within or below budget.** Variance analysis using cost variance and cost
performance index, plus achievement of financial targets with vendors per the signed contract.

**F3. Deliverables validated according to plan.** Earned value management applied, plus other
metrics as defined by the organisation.

**F4. Value created as investment, or as another defined kind of value.** Measured against the
success criteria defined for this project — which may be investment or potential future value
rather than in-period financial return.

**F5. Financial visibility achieved.** Trend or graphical analysis in use, with forecasting
performed.

## Stakeholders — Table 2-9, p. 78 (5 checks)

**ST1. Stakeholder engagement maintained.** Feedback collected through interviews or surveys,
periodic alignment on project objectives, and indicators such as Net Promoter Score.

**ST2. Risk responses identified and successfully implemented.** Issue log, risk register and
stakeholder register reviewed together for assessment.

**ST3. Stakeholder agreement with project objectives achieved.** Number of change requests to
requirements reviewed, and feedback collected on increments.

**ST4. Communications management performed.** The communications management plan is reviewed
periodically and re-tailored to stakeholder needs.

**ST5. Project plans integrated with suppliers' perspectives and work outputs.** The project
management plan aligns with the vendor's way of working.

## Resources — Table 2-10, p. 92 (7 checks)

**R1. Shared ownership of the project.** All team members know the vision and objectives; the
team owns the deliverables and outcomes.

**R2. High-performing team in place.** The team trusts one another and collaborates, adapts to
changing situations, and is resilient under challenge. Members feel empowered; the project
manager empowers and recognises them while delivering results.

**R3. Effective resource utilisation.** Actual versus planned resource usage is effectively
managed.

**R4. Acceptable resource-downtime percentage.** Total downtime against total planned resource
time is at an acceptable level.

**R5. The team is productive.** Units produced versus hours worked, or cost of work performed
versus scheduled time, at acceptable levels, with KPIs reviewed regularly.

**R6. Efficient management of physical and virtual resources.** Materials used, scrap
discarded, and rework volumes indicate resources are used efficiently.

**R7. Resource procurement management conducted.** Procurement processes, efficiency checks,
ordering, manufacture and delivery tracking all successfully managed.

## Risk — Table 2-11, p. 102 (7 checks)

**RK1. Awareness of the project environment.** Technical, social, political, market and
economic contexts incorporated when evaluating uncertainty, risks and responses.

**RK2. Proactive exploration of and response to uncertainty.** Risk responses aligned with
project constraints — budget, schedule, performance.

**RK3. Capacity to anticipate threats and opportunities, and understand consequences of
issues.** A process for identifying, assessing, documenting and responding to risks exists and
is well understood by the project team.

**RK4. Delivery achieved with minimal negative impact from unknown events.** Reserves in place
and used, scheduled dates met, budget performance within variance thresholds.

**RK5. Opportunities to improve performance and outcomes are realised.** Established mechanisms
used to identify, leverage and track the realisation of opportunities.

**RK6. Contingency reserves used effectively.** The team proactively prevents threats, limiting
reserve consumption while maintaining alignment with objectives.

**RK7. Resilience and rapid recovery developed throughout.** The team is aware of the
organisation's business continuity or emergency response plan; a project continuity plan exists
where applicable; a management reserve covers unknown risks; and in a crisis the team can
quickly adjust structure and processes to adapt to new constraints.

## Quantitative subset — trackable over time

Scope Definition Accuracy % · Scope Creep % · Requirements Stability % · cost variance · cost
performance index · full EVM suite · TCPI · ROI · NPV · IRR · capex/opex position · actual vs
planned resource usage · downtime ÷ planned resource time · units per hour · cost of work
performed vs scheduled time · scrap and rework volumes · Net Promoter Score · change request
volume against requirements.

Everything else is evidence-based rather than numeric. **Do not force a number onto a
qualitative check** — "shared ownership of the project" degrades into nonsense as a percentage.
